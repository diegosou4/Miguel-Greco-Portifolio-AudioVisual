# Home — cinematic editorial portfolio

Approved first delivery: React, built directly in code. The user approved the cinematic editorial direction; this surface brief preserves its concrete expression without making its page sequence a global design-system rule.

## First viewport

A real opening film fills the available viewport below the quiet header. The headline “Um olhar. Muitas histórias.” overlays the film with clear text contrast, an action to view the opening film, an action to explore work and a visible play/pause control. There is no autoplay sound. A subtle scroll-driven inset closes around the frame; reduced motion leaves it fixed and offers manual playback over the poster.

## Sequence

Opening film → selected projects → complete filtered archive → brand and wedding pathways → short professional story → WhatsApp contact and optional briefing form.

The initial portfolio presents six selected films. Visitors can expand to the complete thirteen-project archive or select a category. Asymmetric unboxed imagery connects the work to an editorial treatment; mobile keeps a sequential flow with alternating side offsets. Brand and wedding pathways use real source project frames and direct visitors toward relevant films.

## Content and contact

Work titles, images, roles and links are grounded in the incumbent portfolio. Project details show participation and a playable film or source archive, followed by an original-file fallback. The biography describes Miguel's audiovisual practice and experience at Retriz. WhatsApp contact remains direct; the optional briefing prepares a message for the visitor to review and send.

## Delivery facts, not visual invariants

Some source covers contain baked-in captions; cleaner source frames can replace them without changing the design system. A user-supplied local 25-second portrait video (576 × 1024) accompanies the biography beneath Miguel’s name, captioned “Além da ilha de edição.” It replaces the former photo pair. The video sits in the left heading column beside biography text on desktop; on mobile it is centered before the biography. A white, slightly rotated Polaroid frame presents the 9:16 video with the original travel photograph as its initial poster. The centered play overlay starts playback with audio; native controls then provide playback and volume adjustment. The video remains paused on arrival, so the site opens silently. It preloads metadata only and does not autoplay. Source-cover conditions are not a mandate to use captioned covers. Google Drive hosts source films. This delivery has no publishing CMS backend. These integration facts belong to this surface and product scope, not reusable visual rules.

## Mobile contact extension

The header and mobile menu expose WhatsApp directly. A floating contact shortcut follows the implemented visibility rule: it appears when no more than 15% of the hero intersects the viewport, and hides while contact, footer or a film player is visible. Small screens use a 56px icon-only circle with an accessible name and safe-area clearance. Main contact and prepared-message actions retain visible text beside the brand SVG. Landscape layout protects header and film text from device insets.

## Biography journey detail

Below the biography contact link, a quiet static dotted route connects twelve SVG icons: plane, car, camera, laptop, wedding rings, clapper, microphone, course cap, filmstrip, motion, boat and sun. Charcoal at 0.24 opacity over paper keeps the decoration subordinate to the story. Dedicated space beneath the text avoids overlap; the route adapts from 270px desktop height to 240px tablet and 220px mobile. It is decorative, hidden from assistive technology and does not intercept pointer interaction.
